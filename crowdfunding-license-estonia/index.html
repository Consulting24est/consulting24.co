Estonia Crowdfunding License (ECSPR) 2026 — €37,000 Fixed, EU-Wide Passport

By Mardo Soo, Founder & CEO, Consulting24 (X24Consulting OÜ) · Updated 2026-08-01

Short answer: Estonia's crowdfunding licence is an ECSPR authorisation issued by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority): €1,000 state fee, own funds of €25,000 or an insurance alternative, statutory decision within 3 months of a complete application — realistically ~6–9 months elapsed. Consulting24 prepares and files the full application: fixed €37,000 fee. One licence covers the whole EU via a free Article 18 notification.

A crowdfunding license under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (ECSPR) lets one company facilitate business loans and place securities for projects raising up to €5 million each — in every EU member state, from a single home authorisation. Estonia is our direct-delivery jurisdiction for it: we are an Estonian firm, we prepare the file in Estonian (the only language Finantsinspektsioon accepts), and we file it in Tallinn through the regulator's own portal.

The numbers make Estonia's case bluntly. The application fee is €1,000, flat — against €2,500 plus recurring levies in Latvia and up to €75,000 of hourly billing in the Netherlands. The prudential requirement is own funds of €25,000, replaceable with an insurance policy under ECSPR Article 11. And per ESMA's market report on 2024 data, roughly 78% of amounts invested through Estonian-authorised providers already come from abroad — the clearest evidence anywhere in the EU that an Estonian seat is built for cross-border business, not a local niche.

Step-by-Step Process and Timeline

Statutory clocks first, per the regulator's own page: Finantsinspektsioon confirms completeness within 25 working days and must decide within 3 months of a complete application. In practice, Q&A rounds stretch total elapsed time to roughly 6–9 months from first filing, and timelines can extend. Here is the whole path from zero:

  1. Initial consultation — business model, service scope (lending, securities placement, or both), honest fit check. If Estonia is wrong for you, we say so.
  2. Estonian OÜ registration — 1–5 business days; e-Residency works for founding and remote administration.
  3. Onboarding and information gathering — 1–2 weeks; team CVs, shareholder documentation, payment-flow design.
  4. Documentation build — 4–8 weeks. Our base documentation has been refined through two completed rounds of Finantsinspektsioon review in a live application, which may shorten the documentation-preparation phase by up to ~3 months — never the regulator's clock.
  5. Filing — in Estonian, via the Finantsinspektsioon application portal (mandatory route since 18.03.2026).
  6. Review and Q&A — 25-working-day completeness check, then the 3-month statutory decision window on a complete file; realistically ~6–9 months elapsed including question rounds.
  7. Licence + Article 18 passporting — notify Finantsinspektsioon of each target country: €0 fee, services can start at the latest 15 calendar days after notification.

Requirements: Team, Capital, Substance

The application file tracks Article 12 of ECSPR and the 18 information categories of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2112. What Finantsinspektsioon actually looks for:

  • Team: a salaried CEO and CFO; an AML officer; a data protection (compliance) officer; and at least one Estonian-resident management board member (local director) — a must-have; IT may be outsourced.
  • Prudential safeguard: own funds of €25,000 — or one quarter of prior-year fixed overheads if higher — satisfiable by own funds, an EU-covering insurance policy, or a combination (ECSPR Art 11).
  • Vehicle and substance: an Estonian OÜ with a registered office, genuinely managed from Estonia. Foreign ownership can be 100%; the operating team cannot be 100% remote.
  • Payments: the platform should not touch client money without a PSD2 licence — the standard model is a declared partnership with a licensed PSP/EMI (Art 10(5)); see our Estonian payment institution licensing page for the partner side.

The documentation set itself, in plain English: programme of operations; governance and internal controls; ICT and data-security description; operational risk assessment; prudential-safeguard proof; business continuity plan (how loans keep being serviced if the platform fails); management and 20%+ shareholder identity with clean-record proof and CVs; conflict-of-interest rules; outsourcing arrangements; complaint handling; the payment-services setup; KIIS verification procedures; and non-sophisticated-investor protections (entry test, loss simulation, the €1,000/5% threshold flow, 4-day reflection period). We maintain the complete Estonian-language package in-house — 30+ documents including the Article 18 notification template.

What the Licence Covers — and When Estonia Is NOT the Right Fit

Covers: lending-based and investment-based crowdfunding for business projects up to €5,000,000 per project owner per rolling 12 months (ECSPR Art 1), plus an Art 25 bulletin board for secondary interest — without a matching engine. Does not cover: consumer credit, donation/reward crowdfunding (no licence needed), raises above €5M (prospectus regime), and crypto-asset offerings — tokenized instruments fall under MiCA/CASP rules instead.

Be honest with yourself before spending €38,000 all-in. Estonia is not the right fit if: your model is consumer lending (that is national consumer-credit law, not ECSPR); your project owners need more than €5M in 12 months (prospectus/MiFID territory); or you are raising with tokens (see our Estonia crypto license guide). Telling wrong-fit founders “no” early is cheaper for everyone.

Frequently asked questions

Who issues the crowdfunding license in Estonia — Finantsinspektsioon or the FIU?

Finantsinspektsioon, the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority, is the single competent authority under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (ECSPR). It is not the FIU (Financial Intelligence Unit) — that is Estonia's AML authority, which licensed legacy crypto businesses and has no role in crowdfunding authorisation. Applications go through the Finantsinspektsioon portal (mandatory since 18 March 2026) and must be in Estonian.

How much does an Estonian crowdfunding license cost?

The regulator's processing fee is €1,000. Own funds of €25,000 (or an equivalent insurance policy) are required as a prudential safeguard, not a fee. Consulting24's full application and documentation service is €37,000 fixed, with an optional €3,000 business plan. Ongoing supervision fees are volume-based at 0.001–0.1% of intermediated amounts.

How long does it take to get a crowdfunding license in Estonia?

Finantsinspektsioon confirms completeness within 25 working days and must issue a statutory decision within 3 months of a complete application. Realistically, plan ~6–9 months elapsed from filing including Q&A rounds, and ~6–12 months from company formation to licence. Timelines can extend and rest solely with the regulator.

What is the minimum capital for an Estonian ECSPR license?

The prudential requirement is own funds of €25,000 — or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads, whichever is higher (ECSPR Art 11). It can be met with own funds, with an insurance policy covering the EU territories where offers are marketed, or a combination. Insurance replacing own funds runs roughly €1,000–1,500/month (indicative, from recent client quotes — insurer-dependent).

What does one Estonian licence let me do across the EU?

Everything ECSPR covers — facilitating business loans and placing transferable securities up to €5,000,000 per project owner per 12 months — in all EU member states. Cross-border expansion is a free Article 18 notification through Finantsinspektsioon; you can start serving a new country at the latest 15 calendar days after notifying.

What team do I need in Estonia?

A salaried CEO and CFO, an AML officer and a data protection (compliance) officer. At least one Estonian-resident management board member (local director) is a must-have. IT can be outsourced. Finantsinspektsioon expects the company to be genuinely managed from Estonia — an empty shell run entirely from abroad will not pass.

Can I use e-Residency to get an Estonian crowdfunding license?

e-Residency lets founders establish and administer the Estonian OÜ fully remotely — company registration takes 1–5 business days. But the licensed platform itself needs real Estonian substance: a local office, genuine local management activity and named responsible persons. Foreign ownership of 100% is fine.

Who currently holds an Estonian crowdfunding license?

As of August 2026 (checked August 2026), Finantsinspektsioon's register lists exactly one authorised provider: Estateguru OÜ, a lending-based platform licensed on 8 May 2023. Crowdestate AS returned its licence in April 2026. No investment-based (equity) crowdfunding platform has ever been licensed in Estonia.

What taxes will my Estonian crowdfunding company pay?

Estonia charges 0% corporate income tax on retained and reinvested profits; tax of 22/78 (~22%) applies only when profits are distributed. The planned 2026 rate increase to 24% was cancelled in December 2025. This makes Estonia one of the most reinvestment-friendly EU bases for a scaling platform.

Does the licence differ for lending vs equity crowdfunding?

No — one ECSPR authorisation covers both service types; you apply for the services you intend to provide in your programme of operations. In Estonia, both licences ever issued went to lending platforms, and the current register holds one lending provider. As of August 2026, no equity platform has ever been licensed here.
