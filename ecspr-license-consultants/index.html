ECSPR License Consultants Compared (2026): Law Firms, Specialists, Pricing

By Mardo Soo, Founder & CEO, Consulting24 (X24Consulting OÜ) · Updated 2026-08-05

Short answer: There is no single dominant ECSPR licensing consultant in the EU — the market splits into four categories. International law firms (Bird & Bird, Osborne Clarke) have publicly documented authorisation mandates but publish no prices. Regional specialists (FIN LAW in Germany, Adam Smith and ECOVIS ProventusLaw in Lithuania, Eesti Firma in Estonia, Projective Group in Benelux) know one regulator deeply; only Adam Smith publishes a package price (EUR 14,600, Lithuania). Software vendors (LenderKit, Crassula) build the compliant platform but defer the authorisation file to legal counsel. Consulting24 delivers the Estonian application directly at a fixed, published EUR 37,000. And the honest baseline: you can also apply to your regulator yourself — the state fee is EUR 1,000 in Estonia and EUR 710 in Lithuania, and no consultant is legally required.

An ECSPR licence — a crowdfunding service provider authorisation under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 — covers lending- and investment-based crowdfunding for business projects up to EUR 5M per project owner per 12 months; consumer lending, larger raises and crypto fall under other regimes. One authorisation from any member state passports across all 27 under Article 18 (Estonia, for example, charges no notification fee — fi.ee). So who actually needs a consultant? Broadly: founders without in-house regulatory counsel; teams filing in a language they do not speak (Estonian via the Finantsinspektsioon portal, Lithuanian at the Bank of Lithuania, German at BaFin); and anyone who wants the file drafted, filed and defended through regulator Q&A by someone who has done it before. If none of that applies to you, you can apply directly to your national competent authority — the application requirements are public and the regulation is directly applicable EU law.

The market you are hiring into

The EU-wide register kept by ESMA lists roughly 230–240 active authorised crowdfunding service providers (ESMA register, checked August 2026; the portal showed 259 ECSP entries of which 237 were marked Active — row counts can include branches and withdrawn authorisations, and ESMA notes national registers remain authoritative for gaps). Authorisations are spread across 20+ member states, which is why no adviser credibly covers every regulator: the useful question is not “who is the best ECSPR consultant” but “who has verifiably worked with my home regulator”.

The registers also show how young this market still is. Estonia's national register currently lists exactly one authorised provider — Estateguru OÜ (fi.ee register, checked August 2026); Crowdestate's licence was revoked in April 2026 at the company's own request (fi.ee news, 14.04.2026). In Germany, Bird & Bird's December 2024 client release describes WIWIN as only the fifth company to obtain a BaFin ECSP authorisation. Thin registers mean most advisers — everywhere — have handled few, if any, complete ECSP authorisations; the comparison below flags who publishes documented mandates and who publishes claims.

How to choose an ECSPR consultant

Six criteria that separate a useful engagement from an expensive one:

  • Match the firm to your home regulator. The Article 18 passport means you file once, in one member state — so a consultant's value is concentrated in that one NCA relationship. A Frankfurt BaFin specialist adds little to a Tallinn filing, and vice versa. Decide the seat first (our Estonia vs Lithuania comparison covers the two Baltic options head-to-head), then shortlist advisers who verifiably work with that regulator.
  • Ask for documented mandates, not adjectives. Bird & Bird and Osborne Clarke publish named ECSP authorisation clients with dates; most other providers publish descriptions of their service instead. Neither is disqualifying — but you should know which kind of evidence you are buying, and you can always ask a firm to name (with permission) a platform it has taken through authorisation.
  • Get the price in writing before you start. Only two providers in this comparison publish figures — Adam Smith (EUR 14,600, Lithuania) and Consulting24 (EUR 37,000 fixed, Estonia). Everyone else quotes per mandate, which is legitimate but makes budget overruns your risk. Ask what is fixed, what is hourly, and what happens if the regulator's Q&A rounds stretch the work.
  • Separate legal advice from software. LenderKit and Crassula solve a different problem — the ECSPR-compliant platform itself (investor categorisation, limits, KIIS workflows). Their own pages defer the authorisation file to legal counsel. You will likely need both an adviser and a platform; do not expect one vendor to be both.
  • Check who signs and who files. The practical difference between an explainer-publisher and a delivery firm is whether they draft the annexes, submit the file to the NCA and sit in the regulator correspondence — or hand you templates. Eesti Firma, Adam Smith and Consulting24 all state they submit and manage regulator communication; confirm the same in any engagement letter.
  • Treat promises of approval or fixed dates with caution. The statutory position is the same everywhere: statutory decision within 3 months of a complete application; realistically ~6–9 months elapsed; timelines can extend. Any provider — ourselves included — only controls the quality of the file, never the regulator's decision or clock.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a consultant to get an ECSPR licence at all?

No. Any company can apply directly to its national competent authority — in Estonia to Finantsinspektsioon (state fee EUR 1,000), in Lithuania to the Bank of Lithuania (state fee EUR 710 via the State Tax Inspectorate). The regulation, the application annexes and the NCA guidance are all public. A consultant adds value when you lack in-house regulatory counsel, when the filing language is a barrier (Estonian, Lithuanian or German, depending on the seat), or when you want someone who has assembled an authorisation file before to draft, file and handle regulator Q&A for you.

How much do ECSPR licensing consultants cost?

Most firms do not publish prices at all. The published data points as of August 2026: Adam Smith (Lithuania) lists a three-stage package totalling EUR 14,600, and Consulting24 (Estonia) charges a fixed EUR 37,000 for direct delivery. International law firms bill by the hour and quote per mandate. Across the market, realistic full-scope advisory budgets run from roughly EUR 15,000 to EUR 60,000 depending on jurisdiction, model complexity and how much of the file you draft yourself — that range is an honest extrapolation from the few published figures, not a survey. State fees and own funds come on top.

How long does ECSPR authorisation take?

The statutory clocks are EU-wide: a completeness check within 25 working days and a fully reasoned decision within 3 months of a complete application (Articles 12(4) and 12(8), Regulation (EU) 2020/1503). In practice: statutory decision within 3 months of a complete application; realistically ~6-9 months elapsed; timelines can extend. No consultant controls the regulator's clock, and any promise of a fixed approval date should be treated with caution.

Should I hire a law firm, a consultancy, or rely on my software vendor?

They solve different problems. Law firms (Bird & Bird, Osborne Clarke, FIN LAW, ECOVIS ProventusLaw) give privileged legal advice and have documented authorisation mandates. Consultancies (Consulting24, Eesti Firma, Adam Smith, Projective Group) package company setup, document drafting, filing and regulator communication, sometimes at a fixed price. Software vendors (LenderKit, Crassula) build the ECSPR-compliant platform itself — investor categorisation, KIIS workflows, limits — and support the process, but their own pages defer the authorisation file to legal counsel. Many applicants end up combining one adviser with one software vendor.

Which country should my ECSPR consultant specialise in?

The one you will file in. An ECSPR authorisation from any member state passports EU-wide under Article 18 (services may start at the latest 15 calendar days after the notification is submitted; Estonia, for example, charges no notification fee), so the home-state choice drives everything: filing language, regulator style, timeline and substance expectations. Publicly documented specialisations in this comparison: Germany (FIN LAW, Bird & Bird, Osborne Clarke), Lithuania (Adam Smith, ECOVIS ProventusLaw), Estonia (Eesti Firma, Consulting24), Netherlands and Benelux (Projective Group).

How many ECSPR-licensed platforms exist in the EU?

Roughly 230-240 active authorised crowdfunding service providers EU-wide, per the ESMA register (checked August 2026; the portal listed 259 ECSP entries of which 237 were marked Active, and row counts can include branches and withdrawn entries). National registers remain authoritative for gaps — Estonia's, for example, currently lists a single authorised provider, Estateguru (checked August 2026).

Can a consultant guarantee my ECSPR approval?

No. Authorisation decisions rest solely with the national competent authority, which must issue a fully reasoned decision under Article 12(8) of Regulation (EU) 2020/1503. A good adviser improves the quality and completeness of the file and manages regulator Q&A; none of the firms compared here — Consulting24 included — can guarantee an outcome, and any provider that promises approval is overstating what the process allows.
