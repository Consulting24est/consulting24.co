Choose between Swiss and Liechtenstein token rules

By Mardo Soo, Founder & CEO, Consulting24 (X24Consulting OÜ) · Updated 2026-10-24

Short answer: Find the best jurisdiction for token launch by comparing Swiss FINMA rules and Liechtenstein's TVTG. Choose the right legal framework for your crypto project.

The best jurisdiction for token launch depends on the token’s rights, distribution model and target markets, not on one universal rule. Switzerland has token categories set by FINMA, while Liechtenstein’s Token Act and TVTG treat tokens as containers for rights and provide for FMA registration. In either jurisdiction, assess the token and the project’s operating model first.

It’s tempting to choose a jurisdiction before technical work starts and leave legal details for later. That can put token design, fundraising terms and entity structure at odds. Start by defining what the token does and which rights it gives holders.

This guide compares Switzerland and Liechtenstein, their frameworks and the factors that should shape your choice. It also covers the steps to take before token development, from classification and distribution planning to selecting the issuing entity. The aim is to choose a jurisdiction that fits the project’s actual token model.

Key Takeaways

  • Classify the token by its rights, utility and distribution model before development starts; the classification shapes the compliance work.
  • Compare Switzerland and Liechtenstein by how each framework treats your token and issuing structure, not by reputation alone.
  • Assess target markets, tax treatment, operating model and distribution plans together before selecting a jurisdiction.
  • Map the issuing entity, investor documents and token launch responsibilities early to reduce legal and operational friction.
  • Use the jurisdiction comparison to identify the legal steps and project structure that need to be settled before building.

Which jurisdictions are best for token issuance?

There is no single regulatory route for token issuance. Compare jurisdictions against the token’s features, target investors and planned activities.

For the U.S., tokenized assets that qualify as securities are subject to the Securities Act of 1933 and Securities Exchange Act of 1934. In the EU, MiCA is rolling out across Europe; Singapore applies the Payment Services Act to crypto activities and the Securities and Futures Act to qualifying tokenized securities; Switzerland uses FINMA token categories; and VARA and ADGM provide licenses for token issuance and exchanges.

Legal, licensing and compliance costs depend on the token’s classification, the activities performed, the application process and the markets served. Before choosing a structure, compare the work involved for the issuing entity, any VASP application, legal analysis and cross-border compliance.

Gibraltar’s Distributed-Ledger Regulation and Tax Rate

Gibraltar is an option to assess when a project wants a jurisdiction with distributed-ledger regulation and a defined tax figure. According to Gibraltar Income Tax Office, Gibraltar is described as having a 15% tax rate. Compare that rate with regulatory requirements, the token’s classification and the project’s intended markets; tax alone doesn’t establish whether the structure fits.

British Virgin Islands Virtual-Asset Regulation

The British Virgin Islands’ Virtual Assets Service Providers Act of 2022 defines how digital value can be traded. That gives founders a named virtual-asset framework to assess, unlike jurisdictions where token issuance may sit outside a token-specific regime. Map the issuer’s planned activities against the Act, then assess how BVI rules interact with requirements in investor and distribution markets.

U.S. Securities Regulation and Uncertainty

In the United States, tokenized assets that qualify as securities fall under the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The classification therefore affects the compliance path, not just the token’s label. The SEC announced Project Crypto on July 31, 2025, to study on-chain market infrastructure and potential rule adjustments. Build the legal analysis around the token’s rights and distribution.

Jurisdictions Without Token-Specific Regulation

Panama and Seychelles are examples of jurisdictions where a Token SPV may be established without token-specific legislation. This can leave founders facing regulatory uncertainty: they still need to assess the general business, financial and cross-border rules that may apply to the issuer and its activities. Compare this approach with regimes that define token or VASP obligations, and document how the project will manage its intended distribution.

What legal pre-launch steps should you execute before ICO token development begins?

Before development, define the token’s rights, utility and distribution model, then map them against target-market rules. Use those findings to choose the issuing structure and compliance work, rather than starting with a preferred place of incorporation.

Token Utility and Compliance

Start with the token’s function. Record whether it provides access to a product, transfers value, represents an investment or combines features. Document holder rights, governance powers, supply mechanics and the token’s role in the protocol. These details inform its classification under each relevant jurisdiction’s rules.

Next, assess how the token will be offered and distributed. Calling it a utility token does not settle its regulatory treatment. Its features, utility and distribution model all matter. Align the white paper, investor agreements and token design with the classification analysis before developers encode rights or transfer controls.

Taxation and Compliance

Assess tax rules for the issuing entity separately from those for token-sale transactions. Compare how potential jurisdictions treat proceeds, transfers and the movement of value. Switzerland, Liechtenstein, the Cayman Islands and the British Virgin Islands are examples to include in a tax-rule review.

Build the analysis into the structure and fundraising strategy. Identify where economic activity occurs, how funds flow and which entity signs investor documents. Include GDPR compliance in the project’s compliance framework when personal data is involved. Record your assumptions so changes to token distribution or business operations prompt a review.

Multi-Jurisdictional Compliance

A token may be issued in one jurisdiction and marketed or used in others. Create a market-by-market matrix covering classification, marketing, investor eligibility and any VASP obligations. The EU and UK have comprehensive frameworks for crypto-asset service providers, while Singapore’s Payment Services Act sets requirements for relevant crypto activities.

VASP application processes and timelines vary substantially by jurisdiction. Some processes can be prolonged, and some jurisdictions may not have approved applicants. Identify the activities performed by the issuer, platform and other entities. Then assign each obligation to the responsible entity and market, rather than assuming one registration covers every operation.

Token Launch Compliance

Before the token sale, define the issuer’s responsibilities and agreements. A Token SPV may launch the minting protocol, sign investor agreements, convert token warrants and side letters, obtain a legal opinion, and arrange exchange listings. Set out ownership and approval rights among founders, developers and investors.

Use this sequence: finalize token rights and classification; select the issuing entity; prepare investor documents and any required applications; then align launch mechanics with the legal analysis. A SAFT can provide for future token issuance, while a token sale agreement may support additional emissions by a mature project. Early launches can create legal and operational risks, so match readiness to the project’s stage. For help aligning structure and token design, review token launch strategy advice.

Jurisdiction selection for token issuance

Choose the issuing jurisdiction to fit the token, entity and fundraising plan. A founder raising both equity and token capital needs a structure that can support both, while a decentralized protocol may require a different governance and issuer model.

Start with the project’s operating map. Identify where the issuer is formed, where development takes place, where investors are approached and where the token will circulate. An offshore issuer paired with an onshore development company is a common structure. It separates issuance from technical operations, but each entity still needs a clear role and contract chain.

Use these criteria to compare candidate jurisdictions:

  • Regulatory fit: Map token rights and issuer activities to local rules. Switzerland, Liechtenstein, the Cayman Islands and the British Virgin Islands are examples to assess for rules on token-sale taxation.
  • Funding flexibility: Early-stage crypto businesses may need support for both equity-based and token-based funding. Check whether the issuer structure can accommodate both without conflicting investor rights.
  • Execution capacity: Compare access to legal, structuring and other professional services, alongside regulatory procedures and ongoing obligations.
  • Market credibility: Consider how investors, institutional counterparties and distribution partners view the jurisdiction and entity structure.

For a project with centralized governance, a regular limited liability company may serve as the issuing entity. An AG in Switzerland and an LTD in the Cayman Islands are examples. The key question is whether the entity can sign the token contract, take responsibility for issuance and fit the project’s governance model.

For a Layer 1 blockchain, one example is a Swiss Association combined with a UAE development company. That separates a foundation-style governance structure from the team building the protocol. It also makes responsibilities easier to assign across the issuer, developers and investors. The structure must still match the token’s actual rights and operating model.

For a DAO, consider whether a DUNA structure fits the project’s governance and launch plan. For institutional fundraising, prioritize clear issuer duties, investor agreements and decision rights. A neutral jurisdiction may also help with planning distribution channels and a regulatory strategy for key markets, but it does not replace those markets’ rules.

  1. Define the token’s rights, governance and fundraising method.
  2. Map issuer, development and distribution activities by jurisdiction.
  3. Compare regulatory fit, tax treatment, professional services, structuring costs and reputation.
  4. Select the entity model, then align contracts and investor rights with that structure.

The strongest choice is the jurisdiction and entity combination that supports the project’s capital plan, gives investors structural clarity and assigns responsibility across operating locations.

Switzerland’s token categories and Crypto Valley

Switzerland combines a defined token-classification approach with a broader crypto business environment. Compare its legal framework with tax treatment, licensing needs and access to professional services in relevant markets.

FINMA sets clear token categories, so begin by documenting the token’s features, rights and distribution model. Switzerland’s DLT Act recognizes tokenized securities as legally enforceable, giving projects issuing them a specific legal framework to assess. Other token types still need to be classified according to their features. Crypto Valley can inform your view of the commercial environment, but it does not replace regulatory analysis.

For a Swiss project, map these factors before selecting an entity:

  • Token status: Identify the token’s features and how FINMA’s categories may apply.
  • Investor rights: Align tokenized securities and related agreements with the rights holders receive.
  • Cross-border plans: Switzerland is outside the EU. Some Swiss crypto businesses align with MiCA standards for cross-border operations and EU investor collaboration.
  • Operating model: Separate the issuer’s responsibilities from development and other project activities.

Tax treatment can also distinguish jurisdictions. The British Virgin Islands has a 0% tax rate for corporations, capital gains, and withholdings. That figure is not a complete assessment of a token project’s obligations. Consider the issuer’s activities, transaction structure and the markets where the project operates.

Singapore offers a different mix of financial regulation and crypto taxation. It has a 0% capital gains tax on crypto, while the Payment Services Act requires businesses to obtain appropriate licenses and meet specified compliance requirements for crypto activities. MAS is Singapore’s financial regulator. Assess the licensing implications of the actual business activities, not just the location of incorporation.

Professional services affect execution across borders. Compare access to legal, corporate and financial expertise, then assess whether those specialists can support the issuer’s structure, token documentation and target-market analysis. Switzerland, Singapore and the British Virgin Islands each call for a project-specific review. A jurisdiction’s reputation alone does not show whether its services fit a particular issuance.

Crypto-friendly business environments can support different operating priorities. Switzerland has promoted crypto as a payment method since 2016, while Singapore announced plans in 2024 to commercialize asset tokenization and move from sandbox trials toward full adoption. These examples signal distinct policy directions, not identical licensing or tax outcomes.

Choose Switzerland when its token categories and legal framework fit the token’s rights and investor plan. Compare Singapore for its licensing and tokenization direction, and the British Virgin Islands for its stated tax treatment. Then assess each option against access to professional services and the project’s cross-border markets.

Liechtenstein’s Token Act and Token Container Model

Liechtenstein’s framework uses the Token Act and Blockchain Act (TVTG) to address tokens and blockchain-based services. Under the Token Container Model, a token is a container for rights. For founders, the practical first step is to identify those rights before choosing an issuer, drafting agreements or building token functions.

Start by listing what each token represents. It may carry a claim, access right, participation right or another contractual entitlement. Map each right to its holder, responsible entity and relevant agreement. Then check that the token’s technical design accurately represents those rights. A mismatch between code and contract can create operational and legal problems.

The TVTG treats tokens as containers for rights and provides for FMA registration, making registration an explicit part of the framework to assess. That does not mean the token container structure resolves every project activity. Consider the issuer’s role, the services it performs and how tokens move through the project’s blockchain infrastructure.

Use this review sequence:

  1. Describe each token right in plain language and identify its contractual basis.
  2. Map the issuer, token holders, protocol and any service entities to those rights.
  3. Assess whether the project’s activities require FMA registration under the TVTG.
  4. Align token mechanics, investor documents and governance responsibilities before development.

Liechtenstein and Switzerland present their frameworks differently. Liechtenstein’s TVTG emphasizes the token as a container for rights and provides for FMA registration. Switzerland’s FINMA sets clear token categories, while its DLT Act recognizes tokenized securities as legally enforceable. Compare the frameworks against your token’s rights, entity design and operating activities, not a country’s reputation alone.

If the project plans an exchange function, assess exchange activity separately from token issuance. Record which entity issues the token, which entity operates each service and where users are targeted. This activity-based review helps identify whether the structure needs regulatory analysis beyond the Token Container Model.

Liechtenstein may suit a project whose legal design benefits from mapping rights into tokens under the TVTG framework. Switzerland may be more relevant when FINMA’s token categories and the DLT Act’s treatment of tokenized securities fit the project. In either case, settle rights, contracts, registration analysis and governance before blockchain development locks in technical assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

Which jurisdictions are most popular for launching a crypto token?

Founders commonly compare Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Singapore, the Cayman Islands, Malta, the British Virgin Islands, Dubai and Abu Dhabi. No single jurisdiction is best for every token launch. Shortlist locations based on the token’s rights, target investors and issuance structure. For example, a security-token project may prioritize frameworks for tokenized securities, while a protocol fundraising through a Token SPV may focus on issuer structures and cross-border distribution.

How do U.S. securities laws apply to token launches and token sales?

In the United States, tokenized assets that qualify as securities are subject to the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The token’s rights, economic function and distribution model inform the analysis. An offshore issuer shouldn’t assume its place of incorporation settles U.S. exposure if its offering targets U.S. investors or otherwise engages U.S. markets. Assess the offer and marketing plan alongside the entity structure.

Which jurisdictions require token issuers to register or publish a white paper?

Requirements depend on token classification, issuer activities and target markets. MiCA is a European framework for crypto-assets, while Liechtenstein’s TVTG provides for FMA registration. Registration and white-paper duties are separate questions, so map each token type and service against the relevant jurisdiction’s rules. Before the offering begins, prepare disclosures that reflect the token’s actual rights, distribution and risks.

What are the tax implications of launching a token in different jurisdictions?

Tax treatment depends on the jurisdiction and the activity or transaction involved. The British Virgin Islands has a 0% tax rate for corporations, capital gains, and withholdings; Singapore has a 0% capital gains tax on crypto; Gibraltar is described as having a 15% tax rate, according to Gibraltar Income Tax Office. Compare these figures with the issuer’s structure, token-sale transactions and operating markets. A headline rate alone doesn’t determine the project’s overall tax position.

How long does it take to establish a token-issuing entity and obtain required approvals?

Entity formation and regulatory approval involve separate timelines. Businesses can be established in the Cayman Islands within 48 hours if they submit the required paperwork, while BVI establishment can take several days after customer due diligence. VASP application processes differ substantially, and some may be prolonged. Build the launch schedule around document preparation, due diligence and approval steps, not just the incorporation date.

How do jurisdictions differ on AML, KYC, sanctions, and financial-promotion rules for token launches?

Rules vary by activity, entity and target market. Map investor onboarding, transfers, sanctions screening and promotional materials against each jurisdiction where the token will be offered or used. For example, if one entity issues the token and another promotes it, assign responsibility for customer checks and campaign review. This market-by-market matrix helps founders compare the practical compliance workload before selecting the best jurisdiction for token launch.

What are the risks of using an offshore entity for a token launch targeting U.S. users?

An offshore entity does not, by itself, remove potential U.S. securities-law exposure. If a token qualifies as a security, U.S. securities laws may remain relevant to an offering involving U.S. users. Risks include gaps between entity documents and actual distribution, unclear responsibility for investor communications, and late identification of restrictions. Before finalizing token terms or promotions, decide whether U.S. users are in scope and align the offering plan accordingly.

What are the token issuance and crypto licensing requirements in the U.S., EU, Singapore, Switzerland, and UAE?

In the U.S., tokenized assets that qualify as securities are subject to the Securities Act of 1933 and Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The EU is rolling out MiCA; Singapore applies the Payment Services Act to relevant crypto activities and the Securities and Futures Act to qualifying tokenized securities. Switzerland uses FINMA token categories, while VARA and ADGM provide licenses for token issuance and exchanges.
