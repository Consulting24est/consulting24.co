How to Register a Crypto Exchange and Choose Where to Incorporate

By Mardo Soo, Founder & CEO, Consulting24 (X24Consulting OÜ) · Updated 2026-10-24

Short answer: Learn how to register crypto exchange company entities, pick the best jurisdiction, navigate licensing and compliance, and secure banking before launch.

Choosing where to register a crypto exchange starts with its operating markets and planned services. To understand how to register a crypto exchange company, first distinguish company incorporation from financial authorization: according to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, certain crypto companies must register as a Money Services Business in the United States.

It’s easy to focus on the company filing and leave licensing, banking, and tax registrations for later. That can result in a business structure that doesn’t fit its customers or exchange model. Estonia may be worth considering, but incorporation alone does not determine which regulatory requirements apply.

This guide explains how to compare jurisdictions, define your exchange’s activities, incorporate, prepare for banking, and handle tax registrations. It also covers Estonia’s crypto-business framework and the practical work involved in preparing an application. Use it to compare jurisdictions and identify requirements to address before launch.

  • Choose a jurisdiction by matching your target markets and exchange services to its incorporation and licensing requirements.

  • For Estonia, define the exact services first, then align the business plan, compliance procedures, and application materials with that model.

WHERE TO REGISTER A CRYPTO EXCHANGE?

Choose a jurisdiction by matching your customers and exchange activities to an appropriate licensing route. Incorporation creates the legal entity, but it does not authorize every service the company may offer.

  1. Map your target markets, then register the entity where your operating plan supports compliance. U.S. filing fees and processing times vary by state. Include a registered agent in your formation plan, since the agent receives official legal documents for the company. Before accepting payments or processing transactions, map each customer location against the licensing rules that may apply.
  2. Classify the assets and products before selecting a U.S. license. The SEC requirements depend in part on whether an exchange trades digital assets deemed to be securities. An online platform bringing together buyers and sellers of those assets must register as a national securities exchange unless an exemption applies. Regulation ATS may provide an alternative route for a platform that registers as a broker-dealer and files Form ATS. Assess derivatives and other commodity-related activity separately for potential CFTC obligations. For securities offerings, identify applicable disclosure and financial-statement requirements, including whether filings such as Form S-1 or Form 20-F apply.
  3. Determine whether your activities trigger federal and state registrations. According to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, certain crypto companies must register as a Money Services Business. State money transmitter licensing can also apply: the stated market assessment is that 49 states and Washington, D.C. require a Money Transmitter License for crypto exchanges. A U.S. exchange may need a license in every state where it offers services. New York has stringent requirements, including the BitLicense.

  4. Review Operating and Compliance Requirements

    Document how customers deposit funds, trade assets, and withdraw. Set out AML, KYC, and sanctions controls for each flow, including who reviews alerts and escalates concerns. Compare capital, bonding, and insurance requirements by state and license type, as these vary by jurisdiction. Before launch, map cybersecurity, custody, access controls, and consumer disclosures to the exchange’s actual functions.

  5. Assess Legal Risks, Costs, and Timing

    Keep the exchange closed to customers until the required registrations and licenses are in place. Operating without a required approval creates legal and enforcement risk; the consequences depend on the activity and jurisdiction. Budget and schedule by state, not by a single national estimate. New York approvals average 10 months; licensing in smaller states often takes under 4 months.

Bank account opening and authorized capital

  1. Define the account’s purpose before approaching a bank. Distinguish operating funds, customer payments, trading proceeds, and share capital in your financial plan. Explain how the platform handles deposits and withdrawals, whether it controls customer wallets, and how transactions move between accounts. Banks assess crypto businesses cautiously, so a clear description of fund flows helps explain the proposed activity.
  2. Prepare a consistent banking file with formation records, ownership details, a business plan, expected transaction types, and operational policies. Explain how the exchange’s services relate to the proposed account activity. For example, distinguish customer fiat payments from company revenue and state whether the platform holds assets or transfers them to an external wallet. Inconsistencies between the business model and account activity can complicate onboarding.
  3. Set authorized capital according to the license route, not an arbitrary target. Capital requirements depend on the license type, so identify the relevant license first. Then determine how the company will fund the required capital and document its origin. A bank may request source-of-funds records for authorized capital. Prepare ownership and financial documents that show a clear trail from the contributing owner to the company account.
  4. Plan the capital deposit as a distinct corporate transaction. The company needs a bank account to deposit its share capital. Coordinate account opening, capital funding, and licensing documents so each record reflects the same entity and funding purpose. Keep proof of transfers and supporting records with the company files. If your model includes control of customer assets, define the custody arrangement separately from corporate capital. A wallet and custody license may be relevant to that operating model.
  5. For a U.S. company, obtain an Employer Identification Number (EIN) for business administration. U.S. businesses use an EIN to open bank accounts, hire employees, and file taxes. The IRS usually issues it immediately after an online application. Save the issued number with the formation and banking records, and use consistent company details across applications. Differences in the legal name, ownership, or business description can cause avoidable delays.
  6. Build the account-opening sequence around the exchange’s actual operations. First, finalize the entity and ownership information. Next, document expected payments, trading flows, capital sources, and wallet responsibilities. Then prepare the bank application and licensing materials from the same business model. If the exchange serves customers nationwide, map how funds move through the structure and identify which entity receives each payment. This creates a coherent banking file without assuming account approval is automatic.

Company incorporation and tax registrations

  1. Choose the legal entity and jurisdiction before submitting incorporation documents. Match the company’s stated purpose to its exchange activities, customer markets, ownership structure, and licensing plan. For example, specify whether the entity will operate a trading platform, arrange transactions, or hold digital assets. Keep this description consistent across corporate filings, regulatory applications, and banking materials. A mismatch can complicate later reviews.
  2. Prepare formation details in one complete file. Identify the company name, registered office, owners, directors, and business purpose required by the chosen jurisdiction. Use consistent spelling and ownership details throughout. Filing fees and processing times vary by state, so plan around the selected state’s procedures rather than assuming a single national timeline.
  3. Keep incorporation and tax registration separate. Once the company exists, identify its tax residence, where it operates, and which jurisdictions it serves. These factors shape the registrations and filings to address. Create a calendar for relevant authorities and assign responsibility for maintaining records. If the company serves customers in multiple states, map its activities state by state rather than treating the United States as a single tax jurisdiction.
  4. For a U.S. company, apply for an Employer Identification Number through the Internal Revenue Service. An EIN is used to open business bank accounts, hire employees, and file taxes. The IRS usually issues it immediately after an online application. Save the EIN confirmation with the formation records, then use the same legal name and entity details in tax, banking, and regulatory submissions.
  5. Set up transaction records that separate company income from customer activity. Track trading fees, payment flows, asset transfers, and the exchange’s own digital assets separately. The IRS treats cryptocurrency as property, so capital gains taxes may apply to crypto transactions. For U.S. tax purposes, crypto held for less than a year is considered short-term for capital gains purposes. Maintain transaction histories to support the company’s tax reporting.
  6. Review state tax exposure before launch. U.S. sales tax may apply to products or services sold for cryptocurrency, depending on the state. Identify what the company sells, where customers receive it, and how they pay. Keep invoices, transaction records, and the relevant state analysis together. Revisit the assessment when the platform adds services or enters another state, since a change in activity can affect its tax position.

For an India-based entity, India's MCA SPICe+ company incorporation process targets a D/D+1 processing objective. Treat that as a target when planning the filing sequence, not as a substitute for confirming that the submitted formation details are complete and consistent. Keep the incorporation milestone distinct from subsequent tax registrations, and allow the internal launch plan to track each step separately.

Company registration and setup steps

Use this sequence to connect company formation, exchange activities, and the launch plan. Founders searching “how to register crypto exchange company” should treat incorporation and authorization as related but separate workstreams.

  1. Define the proposed activities before drafting the company’s purpose. List each function separately: matching buyers and sellers, processing payments, holding digital assets, or providing wallet access. This scope helps distinguish exchange operations from custody and other services. Use the same description in formation records, business plans, and license materials. If the platform’s functions change, update the operating plan before adding them.
  2. Collect formation details in one working file. Record the legal name, ownership, directors, registered office, and intended business purpose. Keep identity and ownership information consistent across documents. For a state entity, organize registered-agent details and filing records. These records give founders a single reference for corporate filings and later regulatory applications, avoiding conflicting descriptions in separate drafts.
  3. Map each planned service to the relevant authorization before launch. Create a table linking every activity to its customers, transaction flow, assets, and licensing questions. For example, a platform that holds customer assets has a different operating profile from one that only matches orders. Use this distinction to identify the application materials and compliance work needed before the exchange accepts customers.
  4. Set governance responsibilities before the first customer transaction. Assign owners for compliance decisions, customer complaints, transaction monitoring, recordkeeping, and incident escalation. Document who approves platform changes and maintains operating policies. Clear assignments make obligations easier to track and ensure that a new feature, such as wallet functionality, prompts a review of the company’s activities and licensing position.
  5. Prepare a launch file that connects corporate records to actual operations. Include formation documents, the business model, customer journey, payment flows, and procedures that support the declared activities. Keep version dates and approval records for internal documents. Reviewers can compare the company’s stated purpose with how its platform handles transactions and assets. Resolve inconsistencies before submitting applications or opening services to customers.
  6. Build the launch schedule around licensing and operational readiness, not incorporation alone. Sequence entity formation, applications, policy development, platform testing, and customer onboarding. The stated rule is that a licensed company must begin work within six months of receiving its license or the license may be cancelled. Set internal milestones against that period, and do not activate services before required approvals are in place. To register a crypto exchange company, align the entity, authorization, and launch sequence.

Registering a crypto exchange in Estonia

Assess Estonia’s crypto framework against the exchange’s exact activities. Incorporation and authorization are separate parts of the setup.

  1. Define every service before selecting an authorization route. State whether the company exchanges crypto-assets, transfers them, holds custody, or provides another service. The service mix determines the applicable regime, capital, and requirements. For example, an exchange that safeguards customer assets needs a different operational plan from a platform that does not provide custody. Use this activity map in the business plan and application materials.
  2. Assess the current EU authorization framework, not just Estonia’s former permit. As of August 2026, EU crypto businesses operate under MiCA CASP authorization, which covers all 27 EU member states. Estonia’s favorable legislation may make it a suitable incorporation base, but an Estonian company still needs to align its activities with the applicable authorization. A company registration alone does not authorize regulated crypto services.
  3. Build the compliance file around the declared business model. Prepare AML and KYC procedures, risk controls, and internal policies that reflect how the exchange accepts customers, handles transactions, and protects assets. Include security and custody arrangements where those services are part of the model. Make responsibilities clear so reviewers can connect each control to a specific activity and operational owner.
  4. Plan staffing and premises alongside the application. Crypto-license applicants must hire employees for key positions, and some roles must be local. The described licensing process also requires an office rental. Identify the roles needed for the proposed services and show how each supports daily operations, compliance, or customer protection. Treat staffing and office arrangements as application preparation, not post-authorization tasks.
  5. Prepare the application package as a coordinated set. Estonia applicants need a business plan, policies, and application forms. Keep the planned services, risk controls, staffing, office arrangements, and custody model consistent across the documents. If a partner handles wallet infrastructure or another operational function, explain that relationship and its place in the control framework. Inconsistencies between the business plan and policies can make the application less clear.
  6. Separate historical tax information from current structuring decisions. Estonia’s former crypto permit included 0% corporate tax on retained profit; that description relates to the former permit, not a current tax promise. Assess tax registration based on the company’s present structure and operations. For help aligning an exchange model with its licensing route, discuss your crypto exchange structure.

Estonia’s favorable legislation is an advantage, but the exact services determine the regime, capital, and requirements. Tailor AML and KYC procedures, risk controls, and policies to the business model, then prepare the business plan, policies, and application forms.

Frequently asked questions

What is a crypto trading license?

A crypto trading license authorizes specified crypto services in a particular jurisdiction. It is not one universal license: requirements depend on whether a platform exchanges assets, holds customer assets, or handles securities. For example, a business that only develops trading software may have a different regulatory profile from one that executes customer transactions. Incorporation creates the legal entity; licensing covers its regulated activities.

What licenses and registrations are required to start a crypto exchange in the United States?

Requirements depend on the exchange’s services, assets, and customer locations. Certain crypto companies must register with FinCEN as a Money Services Business, and state money transmitter licensing may also apply. If a platform brings together buyers and sellers of digital assets deemed securities, SEC registration as a national securities exchange may be required unless an exemption applies. Assess each product and state separately: no single nationwide exchange license covers every activity.

How do you register a cryptocurrency exchange with FinCEN as a money services business?

First, determine whether the exchange’s activities make it a Money Services Business. According to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, certain crypto companies must register in that category. Map the services, transaction flows, customer relationships, and legal entity before starting the process. Keep descriptions consistent across registration materials and operating records. FinCEN registration addresses federal MSB status, but it does not replace state licensing requirements that may apply.

What are the steps to form a crypto exchange company in the United States?

Form the entity, obtain an EIN, and map applicable registrations before serving customers. Founders asking how to register a crypto exchange company should first choose a state based on intended markets and services, then file formation documents and appoint a registered agent. Next, obtain an EIN from the Internal Revenue Service for banking, hiring, and tax filings. Finally, match federal and state requirements to the exchange’s actual activities and customer locations.

What cybersecurity, custody, and consumer protection requirements apply to crypto exchanges?

Map cybersecurity, custody, access controls, and consumer disclosures to the exchange’s actual functions before launch. Define custody arrangements separately: a wallet and custody license may be relevant if the exchange controls customer assets.
