BVI vs Panama for a Crypto Company: Which to Choose in 2026

By Mardo Soo, Founder & CEO, Consulting24 (X24Consulting OÜ) · Updated 2026-06-13

In 2026, two popular offshore destinations remain the British Virgin Islands (BVI) and Panama. Both offer attractive tax regimes and business-friendly environments, but they differ significantly for crypto regulation, licensing, and operational practicalities. Overview: BVI vs Panama for Crypto Companies

The British Virgin Islands (BVI) and Panama are both renowned offshore financial centres. BVI has long been a favourite for holding companies and investment funds, while Panama offers a strong services sector and the US dollar as its official currency. For crypto companies, the key differences lie in licensing requirements, capital costs, tax treatment, and the ease of opening bank accounts.

In BVI, crypto activities fall under the Securities and Investment Business Act (SIBA) and the Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) regime, which is still evolving. Panama, by contrast, has no dedicated crypto licence; crypto firms register a Panama company and comply with AML/CFT under Law 23 of 2015, reporting to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF). A draft framework is pending but not yet law. As of 2026, Panama offers a flat fee of EUR 6,000 for a company plus AML programme, making it one of the most cost-effective options globally.

Both jurisdictions are tax-neutral, but Panama's simpler setup and lower costs often tip the balance for crypto startups. BVI remains popular for fund structures and holding companies, but for active crypto service providers, Panama's framework is more straightforward.

Who Needs Crypto Authorisation in BVI or Panama?

Any business that provides virtual asset services - such as exchange, custody, transfer, or payment processing - must be properly set up in both jurisdictions. The specific trigger activities include:

  • Operating a cryptocurrency exchange (centralised or decentralised)
  • Providing custodial wallet services
  • Facilitating crypto-to-fiat conversions
  • Running an initial coin offering (ICO) or security token offering (STO)
  • Offering crypto lending or staking services

If your business involves any of these, you must obtain the appropriate license. In BVI, the Financial Services Commission (FSC) oversees VASPs. In Panama, there is no crypto licence; firms register with the UAF for AML and operate as a Panama company. Consulting24 advises clients on which license category applies and coordinates the application process where we operate directly (Panama, Estonia, Lithuania) and advises for other jurisdictions.

It is important to note that even if your business is based elsewhere, you may need a license in the jurisdiction where your customers are located. Both BVI and Panama require a local presence, so you must have a registered office and, in Panama, a local agent.

In BVI, the FSC requires VASPs to comply with anti-money laundering (AML) regulations and to have a physical presence. Panama's route is more streamlined, with a single flat fee for a company plus AML programme. Both require strong AML compliance, but Panama's setup is generally faster and more predictable.

For BVI, the FSC has issued guidance on VASPs but has not yet finalised a dedicated regulatory framework. This creates some uncertainty. Panama, on the other hand, has no crypto-specific law yet; a draft framework is pending. Crypto firms comply with AML/CFT under Law 23 of 2015 (Panama's AML law) and register with the UAF. There is no public register of licensed crypto entities because there is no crypto licence.

For a typical crypto exchange startup, the total first-year cost in Panama (company setup + AML programme + compliance) is approximately EUR 12,000-18,000. In BVI, the same setup can cost USD 20,000-40,000. Over time, the annual renewal costs also favour Panama.

Capital Requirement

BVI does not prescribe a statutory minimum capital for VASPs, but the FSC expects a company to have sufficient financial resources to cover operational and compliance costs. In practice, a minimum of USD 50,000 is often recommended to demonstrate substance.

Panama, on the other hand, has no explicit minimum capital requirement for crypto companies under the flat-fee setup. However, the regulator expects the company to maintain adequate funds for its activities. This makes Panama more accessible for startups with limited capital.

For comparison, EU jurisdictions under MiCA require minimum capital of EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000 depending on the service. Panama's lack of a fixed minimum is a significant advantage for early-stage crypto companies.

Tax Treatment

Both BVI and Panama are tax-neutral jurisdictions. BVI imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax on dividends or interest. Panama taxes only income sourced within Panama (territorial taxation), meaning crypto profits from international clients are generally tax-free. However, Panama does have a 7% VAT on local services, and companies must file annual tax returns. Neither jurisdiction taxes cryptocurrency gains if the income is sourced outside the country.

For crypto companies, this tax treatment is highly favourable. However, founders must consider their home country tax obligations. Consulting24 always recommends consulting a tax advisor to ensure compliance with global tax rules.

It is worth noting that Panama has a network of double taxation treaties, though limited. BVI has no treaties. Both jurisdictions require economic substance, meaning you must have real operations (office, staff) to benefit from the tax regime.

Allowed Activities Under Each License

In BVI, the VASP license permits exchange, custody, transfer, and payment services. It does not cover derivatives or securities unless the company also holds a SIBA license for securities. In Panama, there is no crypto licence; a Panama company with an AML/CFT programme can carry out exchange, custody, payment processing, and token issuance. This keeps the structure flexible, letting businesses scale without multiple licences.

Both jurisdictions require AML programs, and money transmission may need separate authorisation. A Panama company can handle fiat on-ramps and off-ramps, which is convenient for exchange platforms.

For example, a Panama company can offer crypto-to-fiat trading pairs directly, while a BVI-licensed exchange may need additional approvals. This makes Panama more attractive for retail-facing platforms.

Step-by-Step Application Process

Panama (direct delivery by Consulting24):

  1. Company incorporation with a local registered agent.
  2. Prepare AML/KYC policies, business plan, and financial projections.
  3. Register with the UAF and implement the AML/CFT programme.
  4. Due diligence and background checks on directors and shareholders.
  5. Company and AML programme ready to operate (typically about 2-3 weeks).

BVI (advisory coordination by Consulting24):

  1. Incorporate a BVI business company with a registered office.
  2. Engage a local compliance officer and legal counsel.
  3. Prepare a detailed application including AML manual, risk assessment, and business plan.
  4. Submit to the FSC and respond to queries.
  5. License approval (6-12 months).

Banking and Payment Services

Access to banking is a major challenge for crypto companies. In BVI, many international banks are hesitant to open accounts for VASPs due to perceived risks. Some local banks accept crypto clients but require high minimum balances and rigorous due diligence.

Panama has a more developed banking sector that is generally open to compliant crypto companies. The country’s use of the US dollar eliminates currency risk. Several Panamanian banks offer accounts to registered crypto companies, and payment processors like Nequi and Yappy facilitate local transactions. Consulting24 can introduce clients to banking partners in Panama.

For BVI, we can recommend banks that accept VASPs, but options are limited. Panama's banking environment is more favourable, with multiple banks actively seeking licensed fintech clients. Additionally, Panama has a growing network of crypto-friendly payment gateways.

Benefits of Panama Over BVI for Crypto

  • Lower cost: Flat EUR 6,000 company-plus-AML fee vs BVI’s variable and higher costs.
  • Faster timeline: about 2-3 weeks vs 6-12 months.
  • No minimum capital: More accessible for startups.
  • Better banking: Easier to open accounts.
  • US dollar economy: Removes forex risk.
  • Simpler setup: a company plus AML programme, predictable and fast.

For these reasons, Panama is often the preferred choice for crypto founders seeking a cost-effective, regulated base. BVI may still be suitable for specific structures, such as investment funds or holding companies, but for active crypto service providers, Panama offers a better value proposition.

Compliance and Trust Considerations

Both jurisdictions require strong AML/KYC programs. Panama's AML rules require a compliance officer, transaction monitoring, and regular reporting. BVI imposes similar requirements but with less prescriptive guidance. Consulting24 helps clients design compliance frameworks that meet local standards and international FATF recommendations.

Trust is built through transparency. Panama does not license VASPs, so there is no crypto licence register; a well-documented AML/CFT programme under Law 23 of 2015 is what demonstrates compliance. BVI also has no public register. For businesses that need to show partners or customers they are compliant, a strong AML programme is key.

Common Mistakes When Choosing Between BVI and Panama

Founders often make these errors:

  • Ignoring substance requirements: Both jurisdictions expect real operations (office, staff). A mailbox company will not pass due diligence.
  • Underestimating compliance costs: Ongoing AML monitoring and audits can be significant. Budget at least EUR 5,000-10,000 annually for compliance in Panama.
  • Choosing based solely on tax: While both are tax-friendly, banking access and regulatory clarity matter more for crypto.
  • Not checking home country tax: Your personal tax residency may trigger additional obligations. For example, US citizens must report worldwide income regardless of where the company is based.
  • Overlooking the timeline: BVI can take a year or more, which may delay your go-to-market. Panama is faster.

Consulting24 advises clients to evaluate all factors before deciding. A consultation can help you avoid these pitfalls.

Alternatives and Comparisons

Besides BVI and Panama, other popular jurisdictions for crypto licensing include Estonia, Lithuania, and Dubai. Estonia offers a well-known license but has higher capital requirements (EUR 100,000) and longer timelines. Lithuania is more affordable but requires a physical office. Dubai (VARA) is a strong option for Middle East markets but has high setup costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between BVI and Panama for a crypto company?

The main difference is cost and regulatory clarity. Panama has no dedicated crypto licence, so you register a Panama company and run an AML/CFT programme for a flat EUR 6,000, with no minimum capital and a fast setup. BVI has variable costs, typically higher, and a longer 6-12 month timeline. Panama's route is simpler and more predictable, making it easier for startups.

Is a Panama crypto setup recognised internationally?

Panama has no crypto licence to issue; crypto firms operate as a Panama company that registers with the UAF for AML under Law 23 of 2015. That structure is recognised by international banks and partners, though acceptance varies. Panama is not on the FATF grey list as of 2026, which adds credibility. However, you should verify with your specific counterparties.

Can I operate a crypto exchange from BVI without a license?

No. BVI requires a VASP license under SIBA for any virtual asset service. Operating without a license can lead to penalties and reputational damage. Always obtain the proper license before offering services.

What are the ongoing compliance costs for a Panama crypto setup?

Annual renewal fees are EUR 3,000-5,000. Additionally, you must maintain a compliance officer (internal or outsourced), conduct regular AML audits, and file reports. Total ongoing compliance costs typically range from EUR 8,000-15,000 per year, depending on transaction volume.

Does Panama require a physical office for a crypto company?

Yes. Panama requires a registered office and a local agent. The regulator expects substance, meaning you should have a physical presence (office, staff) to demonstrate real operations. A virtual office may not suffice.

How long does it take to set up a crypto company in Panama?

Typically about 2-3 weeks, since there is no licence to apply for; you register the company and put an AML/CFT programme in place. The timeline depends on the completeness of your documents. Consulting24 can help by preparing everything correctly upfront.

Can I use Panama as a base for a global crypto business?

Yes. Panama's territorial tax system means only Panama-sourced income is taxed, so profits from international clients are tax-free. Combined with the US dollar economy and good banking access, Panama is an excellent base for global crypto operations.

What happens if I don't meet the substance requirements in BVI or Panama?

In BVI the regulator can revoke your VASP licence or impose fines if you fail to demonstrate substance; in Panama, weak AML compliance can bring UAF penalties. Both expect a physical office, local staff, and active operations. A shell company will not pass regulatory scrutiny.

Is BVI suitable for a crypto hedge fund?

BVI is more suited for investment funds and holding structures due to its established fund regime. For active crypto trading or exchange services, Panama is generally more practical due to lower costs and clearer regulation.

Can Consulting24 help with BVI licensing?

Consulting24 advises and coordinates for BVI licensing but does not deliver directly. We work with local partners to handle the application. For direct delivery, we recommend Panama, Estonia, or Lithuania.

What are the tax implications for US citizens using Panama?

US citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where the company is based. Panama's territorial tax does not exempt US citizens from US taxes. You should consult a US tax advisor to understand your obligations.

How does Panama compare to EU jurisdictions under MiCA?

EU jurisdictions under MiCA require minimum capital of EUR 50,000-150,000 and have stricter compliance requirements. Panama has no minimum capital and lower costs, making it more accessible. However, EU licenses may offer broader recognition within the EU market.
