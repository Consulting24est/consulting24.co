The Bank of England, the Financial Conduct Authority, the Prudential Regulation Authority and the Payment Systems Regulator have completed their annual review of the Memorandum of Understanding on payments, the FCA said. The review found that cooperation and coordination between the Authorities has strengthened and remains effective, and the Authorities concluded that no changes to the MoU are needed at this time.

The Authorities considered the effectiveness and efficiency of their coordination under the MoU. The FCA said this included feedback from industry and regulated firms to determine whether the MoU's principles of coordination and cooperation continue to be met in practice. The review found that cooperation and coordination remain effective, with respondents welcoming tangible progress in how the Authorities work together.

## Examples of collaboration

The FCA cited examples of this collaboration, including work on a range of developments such as the future regime for dual-regulated stablecoin issuers and other innovations in payments, including the work of the Payments Vision Delivery Committee.

To complement the MoU, the Authorities said they have updated their information explaining how their objectives, regulatory remits and key workstreams fit together. They have provided further updates to the information detailing each Authority's regulatory remit and will continue working together to identify areas where greater coordination would benefit the industry. The FCA said this will help firms navigate the regulatory landscape and support a more coordinated and effective approach to payments regulation.

## Consolidation of the PSR

The FCA said this approach is further embedded through the Government's decision to consolidate the PSR into the FCA, meaning greater coordination, clearer responsibilities and a simpler experience for firms.

The document was first published and last updated on 26/11/2026. The FCA also linked to the MoU itself and to related content on roles and responsibilities in payments regulation and the MoU between the Bank, FCA, PSR and PRA.

The review outcome means the existing arrangements under the MoU will continue without amendment. According to the FCA, the Authorities will keep working together to identify areas where greater coordination would benefit the industry, alongside the updated information on their respective remits.

The FCA's statement sets out that the annual review drew on feedback from industry and regulated firms, and that respondents welcomed the tangible progress identified in how the Authorities work together. The Authorities concluded that the MoU's principles of coordination and cooperation continue to be met in practice and that no changes are needed at this time.
